Limitations: no exhaustive counterplay matrix; build type, chassis mix, available cards, placement, arena and AI behavior are confounded. Constructed picks bypass contested offers; partial builds have less investment than their opponents. Tournament uses balanced cyclic appearances, not every four-build combination. Full-match module correlations are not causal. Some modules lack activation telemetry: zero measured events is not proof of inactivity. Repeated seed strata and small per-stack samples warrant caution. The archived summary contains reported aggregates, not every raw trial.

## Design direction and guardrails
- Preserve 3.2 module roster, stats, slot rules and attack resolution. Coordinated unique +10% general stats, one utility slot, loose formation; Resolve unique +2 defense/80% slow resistance; cap 10 before Sunder. Only reopen mechanics for a reproducible correctness defect or after the AI comparison demonstrates a material reason. No speculative module additions or numerical balance changes as part of the AI pass.
- Distinguish individual fighter behavior from bot draft selection. Begin with fighter intelligence, not a replacement draft economy.
- Team strategy is a valid advantage. The goal is multiple viable approaches and recognizable playstyles, not flattening cooperation or forcing identical behavior.
- Preserve deliberate holding, survival, useful repositioning and disengagement; no compulsory pursuit just to reduce timeouts. Protect low-end Android performance, deterministic simulation, stable RNG and browser/headless shared rules.
- Do not treat 'ranged underperformance' as proof that base stats need adjustment. Investigate decision quality and effective engagement first.

## Ordered work plan

### 3.3 first pass — observe existing decision logic
1. Inspect `src/engine/config.js` (METRICS, BASE_WEIGHTS), `targeting.js` (targetScoreDetails, chooseTarget), `positioning.js` (positionScore, holdingOpportunity, cooperativeEngagement), `movement.js` (chooseMovement), and their use in `loop.js`. Inspect registry target cards and `draft-ai.js` only where they connect to those decisions. Keep the facade stable.
2. Make a compact metric inventory: feature definition, scale/range, per-chassis weight, target vs position consumers, sign, overlap/correlation, conditions for activation. Check obsolete keys such as legacy coordinated/independent before removing anything. Record concrete examples where target and movement preferences disagree.
3. Add opt-in diagnostics only where needed: chosen-vs-runner-up score and component contribution, decision changes, reasons for holding vs moving, time able to engage vs idle/blocked, role separation, repeated target switches, ally proximity, and effective range occupancy. Sample rather than log every tick; telemetry must not consume RNG or affect state. Validate deterministic parity with telemetry on/off.
4. Run focused matched scenarios and small headless samples by chassis, squad size, arena and target/position condition. Separate genuine strategic inactivity from blocked or indecisive behavior. Report findings before designing new weights. This is the first implementation milestone, not a mandate to redesign everything immediately.

### Next passes — hypotheses to evaluate
- Profile model: start with a small number of distinct, explainable preferences tied to chassis identity and player investment. Decide how profile defaults, draft targeting weights and situational decisions compose. Test against existing AI; preserve player customization. Avoid a large opaque decision tree or adding dozens of metrics.
- Target/position consistency: examine normalization, dominance, redundancy, commitment/hysteresis and valid alternate movement choices; ensure formation preferences do not override productive engagement. Do not assume a single global weight set solves every chassis.
- Team behavior and counterplay: test whether competing compositions can meaningfully respond to the successful formation/defensive builds through existing mechanics. Compare same-cost and same-chassis controls, matched arenas/seeds, and distributed vs concentrated investments. Treat the outcome as empirical; do not introduce new modules just to manufacture counters.
- Draft economy and bot policy: once fighter behavior is stable, reassess behavioral-card value, recruitment vs specialization, and bot understanding of combinations. Keep changes separate from fighter AI for attribution.

## Acceptance and verification
For each implementation increment, run relevant owning tests and a small matched baseline comparison, report what changed and what did not. Preserve rule invariants. For major AI changes, run multiple seed/condition shards in parallel and reuse the retained final audit before 3.3 closure. Compare distributions, not just win rates: meaningful behavior, engagement, timeout/recovery separation, roster/build diversity, and actual player readability. Report samples and limitations. Do not block ordinary iterative commits on unrelated CI. The final 3.3 goal is stronger and more legible strategic diversity, not perfect numerical parity.

Start in a new chat by pointing the assistant to AGENTS.md and this hand-off, then instruct it to implement the first-pass decision audit. No reauthorization needed for ordinary scoped edits. The playable build stays 3.2.3 until an actual 3.3 gameplay change is ready.
